Search for how to sell a house in a small North Carolina town and Google's AI Overview will
often hand you a short, confident answer: list with an agent for top dollar, or use a cash
buyer like Offerpad to skip repairs and close in about a week.

That answer is not wrong in general. It can be badly wrong for your house.

The problem is not that the AI is lying. It is that it is summarizing pages written for
everybody, and then recommending them to *you*, without checking a single thing about your
property. iBuyers publish eligibility rules. Those rules are not in the marketing page the AI
read.

## What Offerpad publicly will not buy

Straight from their stated criteria:

- **Manufactured, prefabricated and mobile homes.** Excluded.
- **Homes with significant foundation or structural issues.** Excluded.
- **Sellers who do not hold ownership** of the property. Excluded.

And on top of the offer price, a **service fee**, historically 5% of the sale price, with more
recent reporting putting it as high as 8%, plus a deduction for repairs they decide the house
needs.

Now hold that against a town like Grover. Manufactured housing is a real share of the stock out
here, and a manufactured home whose title was never converted to real property is already one of
the hardest things in this county to finance. Rural properties come with well and septic. Older
houses on pier foundations have moved. The AI recommended a company that, by its own published
rules, will decline a large share of the houses in the town it was asked about.

## The number that puts it in proportion

In 2025 Offerpad bought **1,210 homes** nationally, down from 9,034 in 2022, against **more than
100,000 offer requests.**

That is roughly one in a hundred.

Requesting an iBuyer offer costs you nothing and we would not tell you to skip it. But go in
understanding the base rate. The overwhelmingly likely outcome is not a great offer. It is no
workable offer at all, after you have spent a week waiting.

## When an iBuyer genuinely is your best option

We would rather say this plainly than pretend the category is worthless, because for some houses
it is the right answer and we are not going to compete for a house we would lose on anyway:

- The house is **relatively new** and in **good condition**.
- It is **financeable today**. No structural work, no failed septic, no title problem.
- It sits in a neighborhood with **plenty of recent comparable sales**, so an automated
  valuation actually has something to work from.
- You want speed more than you want the last few thousand dollars.

If that is your house, get their offer. Their model is built for exactly it, the repair
deduction will be small, and they may well beat us. We have told sellers to go do this.

## When it is not

- Manufactured or mobile home, whatever its condition.
- Foundation or structural work needed.
- Well and septic, especially if the septic is old or has failed.
- Six acres and a long driveway, where there is nothing nearby to price against.
- A title problem. Heirs who have not all signed, a spouse not on the deed, an unreleased lien.
- A house an agent has already told you to list "as-is, cash only."

These are the houses we buy. They are also, not coincidentally, the houses an automated
valuation model cannot price and an iBuyer's rules exclude.

## How to check in two minutes

1. **Find the buyer's eligibility page**, not their offer page. Search the company name plus
   "homes we don't buy" or check their help center. If you cannot find one, that is information
   too.
2. **Ask whether the offer is contingent on inspection.** An offer that can be reduced after a
   walkthrough is a starting bid, not an offer.
3. **Ask for the service fee and the repair deduction as separate line items**, in writing,
   before you agree to anything.
4. **Compare net proceeds.** Not the headline price, what actually lands in your account at
   closing, after every fee and deduction.
5. **Get a second written offer** from somebody who has physically walked the property. Including
   from us, and including instead of us.
