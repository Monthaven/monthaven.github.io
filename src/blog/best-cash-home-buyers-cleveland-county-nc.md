Most "best cash home buyers" articles are written by one of the buyers and name no competitors.
Ours was, too, [the Gastonia version](/blog/best-cash-home-buyers-gastonia-nc/) describes
categories without naming anybody. That was the cautious choice and it was less useful than it
should have been.

So here is the list, with names. We are on it. Read it knowing that.

**Everything said below about another company comes from that company's own public material**. Their website, their Better Business Bureau profile, their published terms. We are not
characterizing anyone's conduct from rumor, and where we do not know something we say so.

## The genuinely local ones

### J&B Homebuyers, Shelby

The closest cash buyer to most of this county, based in Shelby and operating here for several
years. They are the only company on this list actually headquartered in Cleveland County, and
they have a well-established review history. The Charlotte companies below have more reviews
in total; J&B is the one whose office is in the county you live in.

If you are in Shelby, Kings Mountain or Grover and you are only going to call one buyer, they
are a reasonable one to call. We would rather tell you that than pretend the most established
local operator does not exist.

*We have not published detail on their process here because we would only be describing it
secondhand. Go read their own site.*

### Monthaven Home Buyers, Gaston County (that's us)

We are in neighboring Gaston County and we buy across Gaston, Cleveland and Lincoln. We are
**the newest buying operation on this list**, and we would rather say that outright than pad a
number. Our owner has been a licensed North Carolina broker since 2020, with most of that
experience on the agent side, and is building the buying side now.

What we think we do better than the category: we publish the arithmetic. The
[four-part formula behind every offer](/blog/how-much-do-cash-home-buyers-pay-nc/), the
[real margin on a real flip](/how-it-works/) including the one where a water heater ate it, and

What we do not have yet that others on this list do: years of local closings and a long review

## The national iBuyer

### Offerpad

Offerpad has a page for essentially every town in North Carolina, including ones where it is
unclear they have ever bought anything. Before you spend a week on it, know what their published
rules say:

- **They will not buy manufactured, prefabricated or mobile homes.**
- **They will not buy homes with significant foundation or structural issues.**
- They charge a **service fee** on the sale price, historically 5%, with recent reporting
  putting it as high as 8%, and deduct estimated repair costs on top.
- In 2025 they bought **1,210 homes nationally against more than 100,000 offer requests.**

In rural Cleveland County, where manufactured housing is a real share of the stock and plenty of
properties are on well and septic, those first two exclusions matter enormously. It is also,
notably, the company Google's AI Overview recommends to sellers here, which is
[a problem we wrote about separately](/blog/ai-told-you-to-sell-to-an-ibuyer/).

When are they the right call? A newer, financeable, good-condition house in a neighborhood with
lots of comparable sales. That is what their pricing model is built for, and on that house they
may well beat everyone else on this page. Go get the offer.

## The advertisers

Two more you will see, both buying placement rather than ranking:

**Mark Spain Real Estate** runs paid ads on these searches from a Charlotte office, offering to
buy your house for cash or list it, a two-option model.

**Livlyte** advertises "don't sell cash, get 90% of full market value" with no closing costs and
a 15-day timeline.

We have not worked with either and cannot tell you how those offers land in practice. Two things
are worth knowing generally: a paid ad tells you a company has a marketing budget, not that it
has a track record in your county; and any offer expressed as a percentage of "full market value"
depends entirely on who calculates that value and how. Ask for it in writing, with the
calculation shown.

## What we deliberately have not told you

**Star ratings and review counts.** They change weekly and quoting them would make this page
stale within a month. Look them up yourself, today, and read the one-star reviews and the
company's replies, which are worth more than the average.

**Anything negative about a local competitor.** We know these people operate in a county this
size, and we are not going to characterize their work from the outside. If we have not
experienced something directly, it is not going on this page.

## The honest bottom line

**If your house is in decent condition and you can wait two or three months, list it with an
agent.** You will almost certainly net more than any buyer on this page can offer, including us.
In a town where very little is for sale, a financeable house has real leverage.

**If your house cannot pass a lender's inspection**. Structural work, a failed septic, a
manufactured home whose title was never converted, a title problem with heirs who have not all
signed. Then a cash sale is not a discount you are choosing, it is the realistic route, and the
iBuyers are already out.

Either way: **get more than one written offer.** Ask each buyer for their after-repair value and
their repair estimate, item by item. Compare net proceeds, not headline prices. The gap between
two honest offers on the same house is routinely five figures, and the only way to find it is to
make people put numbers on paper.

That advice costs us deals. We would rather be the page you can check than the one you have to
trust.
